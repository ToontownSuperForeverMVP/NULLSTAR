# Weapon specifications

The three identities and their implementations are original. Part 2 replaced the native particle presentation with authored procedural machinery, custom RenderPearl shaders, original procedural textures and synthesized sound effects (generators in `tools/assets`). No reference-mod code, models, textures, music, sounds or cinematic sequences are incorporated. Gameplay timings below are unchanged and authoritative; presentation is derived from them.

## Shared gameplay

Hold the Orbital Controller in the main hand. Sneak-use or V selects a weapon. Aim at a loaded surface and use/G to designate it; use/G again launches the confirmed strike. Operators are required for destructive activation by default. Designations expire in ten seconds. The engine limits one active event per owner and four simultaneous events by default. Leaving the dimension, disconnecting, dying or unloading the target cancels remaining work.

All timings below are ticks at multiplier 1 and a nominal 20 TPS. Cooldown begins at authorization and survives cancellation/disconnect during the current server session. Phase `COOLDOWN` is the final presentation phase; the cooldown gate can remain active longer. The event stays registered until terrain jobs finish.

## HELIOS ANNIHILATOR

Technical sequence: acquiring 20, charging 60, orbital alignment 40, firing 20, atmospheric entry 30, impact 10, aftermath 100, cooldown 20. Impact cue occurs at absolute tick 170. A gold energy source appears 72 blocks above the target. Rings and a luminous core build during charging/alignment; a descending gold/white column leads into an expanding radial shockwave, warm impact accent and smoke. A rounded, irregular bowl removes the terrain asynchronously. Supported exposed shell materials become basalt, blackstone or coal.

Damage is radial, up to 60 at the center, falling to zero at 1.5 times the effective radius; knockback scale is 1.7. Creative/spectator players are excluded. Radius and depth obey the server caps. Default full candidate volume is 42,025, distinct from actual changed blocks; crater seed affects the changed count.

Implemented presentation (`HeliosScene`): a gold ground marker rises during acquisition. The asymmetric collector descends 34 blocks while charging (warm ground glow, ascending motes, collector-synchronized pulse rings). Alignment runs as a choreographed sequence: twelve collector segments (one deliberately omitted) rotate and extend into lock, five asymmetric segmented solar vanes unfold with staggered timing, two gimballed focusing rings counter-rotate and settle level, three targeting pods swivel onto the confirmed target and paint laser sight lines, eight armoured reactor petals hinge open and a six-blade iris opens under the core. The reactor is layered: white-hot nucleus, plasma envelope, rotating containment shells, converging charge motes, arcs, escaping prominences and flowing spoke conduits, with increasing vibration. Firing adds an aperture flash, twin expanding rings and recoil; the beam is layered (white core, gold body, breathing red-orange envelope, helical traces, radial arcs, travelling shock diamonds, soft halo). Atmospheric entry adds a compressed plasma sheath, upward luminous streaks and pre-impact dust. Impact uses a contact flash, an incandescent fireball, a vertical pillar, three expanding shock rings, a ground pressure wall, terrain-tinted hot debris, sparks, embers, a dust ring and a rising column. Aftermath fades energy rings, pools plasma on the crater floor, keeps smoke and embers rising, and the collector powers down, closes and ascends away.

Shots: `helios_establish` (tilt-up reveal from the player's side), `helios_mechanism` (orbit around the unfolding machine), `helios_discharge` (below the aperture), `helios_follow` (tracks the beam head down), `helios_impact` (ground-level wide), `helios_aftermath` (rising orbital crane revealing the crater). Sounds: `helios.activate/charge/align/fire/descent/impact/aftermath/shutdown` per phase, with client accents `helios.ring_lock` (three locks), `helios.pulse` and a distance-delayed `helios.aftershock`.

## SINGULARITY ENGINE

Technical sequence: acquiring 20, charging 80, firing/attraction 120, impact 15, aftermath 100, cooldown 20. Implosion cue at tick 220. A violet ring surrounds a dark core four blocks above the ground, with inward spiral particles. During firing, the server attracts eligible living entities inside twice the radius every five ticks; velocity is clamped. Implosion creates a deeper ellipsoidal excavation, radius 13 and vertical axis 18, with obsidian/blackstone shell transformations. Damage cap is 45 and knockback 0.7.

Implemented presentation (`SingularityScene`): the event horizon is an opaque black sphere (it writes depth, so it occludes the far side of the disk) inside a soft dark falloff and an uneven photon ring. A tilted accretion disk is drawn as Keplerian bands whose inner rings shear faster, brightened on the side moving toward the camera. A camera-facing lensed image of the disk's far side arcs over and under the horizon. Three tilted dashed accretion rings rotate unevenly and shake apart as the anomaly destabilizes; logarithmic violet ribbons and inward motes trace the attraction, fracture arcs crawl over the horizon, and refractive rim shells (plus an optional lens-shadow when screen distortion is enabled) suggest bending light. During attraction, terrain-tinted fragments and dark crystal shards spiral in deterministically and stretch tidally; dust is dragged inward. In the final quarter the core contracts, a ring of light rushes inward, ambient layers dim and the audio cuts to a deliberate silence. The implosion releases a flash, an expanding violet shock shell, a dark pressure ring, violet ground rings, a short pressure wall and brief twin jets, throwing cold (non-glowing) debris and shards. Aftermath shows a flickering residual anomaly, violet arcs crawling over the deep crater and inward-drifting motes. True screen-space lensing is not implemented (see the release report); the effect is a geometric illusion.

Shots: `singularity_form` (push-in), `singularity_pull` (slow orbit with growing roll ending in a dolly zoom), `singularity_implode`, `singularity_aftermath` (high crane into the crater). Sounds: `singularity.activate/charge/pull/implode/aftermath` and `singularity.collapse`, with accents at destabilization and a low distance-delayed aftershock.

## GLASSFALL ARRAY

Technical sequence: acquiring 20, charging 50, alignment 20, firing/barrage 170, final impact 15, aftermath 100, cooldown 20. Five launches/impacts are authoritative timeline cues. Four smaller projectiles launch at ticks 90/125/160/195, impacting 30 ticks later at offsets (-9,-7), (8,-6), (-7,9), (9,8). A larger center projectile launches at 230 and impacts at 260. Each offset gets a bounded local surface probe. Unloaded offset chunks are skipped without generation; the reserved job slot is released.

Visual trajectories are client-only cyan/white particle streaks; no independently simulated projectile can drift away from the server impact schedule. Four subcraters use 35% of radius/depth/damage, then the center bowl uses radius 16/depth 10. Sand at scorch boundaries becomes glass; stone becomes basalt. Five impact jobs are reserved before launch so a barrage cannot overcommit halfway through.

Implemented presentation (`GlassfallScene`): ground rings mark all five authoritative impact sites during acquisition. The lattice descends and unfolds: an octagonal frame and a square frame counter-rotate and snap into register, four truss arms telescope out with cyan conduits, a counter-rotating lens stack glows beneath the hub, and five launch rails hinge to aim along their impact directions, each holding a glowing crystal (the final rail holds a larger twin crystal). On every `projectile` cue the crystal leaves its rail with a launch flash and follows a deterministic Bézier curve that starts at the rail and ends exactly at the cue's server-resolved impact position (unit-tested), accelerating, oriented along its tangent, with refractive rim shell, inner glow, analytic trail, helical filaments and atmospheric streaks. Each sub-impact varies by seed: flash, sharp hexagonal and secondary shock rings, a crystal dome flicker, erupting crystal spikes that dissolve, shards, dust and debris. The final strike adds an expanding cyan energy dome, a light pillar, five tilted angular shock fragments, sixteen spikes at ULTRA, heavier debris and shards. Aftermath leaves drifting glitter and a cyan haze while the lattice retracts upward.

Shots: `glassfall_reveal`, `glassfall_lattice`, `glassfall_barrage` (tracks each crystal along its real flight curve, pushing in before the final launch), `glassfall_final`, `glassfall_aftermath`. Sounds: `glassfall.activate/charge/align/barrage/final/aftermath` per phase, with per-projectile `glassfall.launch` and `glassfall.whistle`, varied `glassfall.strike` sub-impacts and a distance-delayed low layer for the final impact.

## Shared presentation

Holding the controller shows a holographic ground reticle (rotating rings, calibration ticks, scanning sweep and the selected weapon's bundled effective radius) and a screen reticle with lock contraction, out-of-range warning and designation countdown. Locking plays `ui.lock`; selection plays `ui.select` and shows a compact selector. Owners get letterboxed directed shots; observers keep their own view and see all world effects. Quality presets, accessibility and budgets are described in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## Data and extension rules

Edit bundled JSON or place validated per-ID overrides in `config/nullstar/weapons`. Stage fields are phase/ticks/camera/sound; cues have absolute tick/effect/x/z/radiusScale. Impact fields define geometry, radial damage, knockback and scorch. Presentation fields define color, orbital height, particle quality scale and sound pitch. See `WeaponDefinition` for the exact validation limits.

`/nullstar reload` only succeeds when no strike is active. Invalid combined timing/config values retain the prior settings. The shared server behavior dispatch currently recognizes three behavior IDs; new behaviors should use reusable timeline cues and queue APIs rather than duplicate terrain/network code.
